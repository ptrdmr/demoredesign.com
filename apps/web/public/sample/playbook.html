03 · The "How"

Outreach Playbook

The system your marketing team runs to turn local groups into recurring bookings, with a deliberate bias toward breakfast, lunch, and off-peak day-parts where the venue has open capacity. Every offer, script, and follow-up step is built to be copy-paste ready.

How to Use This Playbook

  1. Pick a segment from the Offer Matrix and grab its recommended hook.
  2. Build a target list of 30–50 real organizations using the Partner Map.
  3. Confirm the offer math with the venue owner, replace every Confirm flag with real numbers (see the checklist on the Plan Overview).
  4. Run the cadence (Touch 1 → Touch 5) using the Master Templates, tailored per segment.
  5. Log everything in the tracker below. No prospect lives in someone's head or inbox.
  6. Review the KPIs weekly. Double down on the segments and offers that book; retire the ones that don't.

Golden rule

The goal is never a single visit. Every first booking is an audition for a standing arrangement. Close the recurring date while you're still shaking hands.

Segment Offer Details

All dollar figures, percentages, and minimums below are smart defaults for a fast food chain, assumptions, not confirmed venue pricing. The venue owner must approve final numbers.

K-12 Schools, "Venue Spirit Night"

Offer: the venue donates 15–20% of sales generated by the school's families during a designated 2.5-hour window. Schedule Monday–Wednesday evening or an off-peak weekday so the donation comes from incremental traffic. Confirm whether takeout counts and whether the % is on food only or total.

Recurring mechanism: Book one spirit night per grading period; name a "Venue PTA Liaison" and give the organizer a thank-you perk.

Youth Sports, "Victory Milkshake Bundle"

Offer: Flat per-player bundle, entrée + fries + signature milkshake at $X/player. Fills weekend mornings/early afternoons; the milkshake is the "trophy."

Recurring mechanism: Lock a standing weekly slot for the season; offer the coach a free meal; ask for an intro to other coaches.

Summer Camps, "Field Trip Lunch"

Offer: Reserved section + simplified group menu at $X/camper, free meals for 1 per 10 chaperones. Fills weekday late-morning/lunch capacity.

Recurring mechanism: Sign a weekly recurring camp day for the summer, 8–10 guaranteed off-peak bookings per camp.

Colleges / Student Orgs, "Group Study Break"

Offer: Group rate for clubs/teams plus a simple group photo or reserved seating block. Optional grab-and-go study-break bundle.

Recurring mechanism: Monthly club social or a semester-long standing reservation; recruit the club's social chair as the repeat contact.

Churches, "After-Service Brunch + Youth Night"

Offer: (1) Reserved Sunday after-service section; (2) a midweek youth-group night with a group meal + shake special.

Recurring mechanism: Standing weekly/biweekly reserved tables, coordinated with the church office's bulletin/announcements.

Senior Centers, "Sunrise Senior Club"

Offer: Weekday breakfast special at a fixed senior price $X, reserved seating, calm early-morning service. One of the highest-leverage segments for the off-peak goal.

Recurring mechanism: A standing weekly breakfast club (e.g. "Tuesdays are Venue Senior Sunrise"), coordinated with the activities director.

Service Clubs, "Your Club's Meeting Home"

Offer: Host their recurring weekly/biweekly meeting, semi-private section, set menu, guaranteed headcount, fast service so they hit their schedule.

Recurring mechanism: Become their official meeting location with a standing weekday breakfast/lunch reservation.

Scouts / Youth Programs, "Kitchen Tour Field Trip"

Offer: Behind-the-scenes restaurant tour tied to a badge/learning theme, plus a group meal at $X/scout.

Recurring mechanism: Make it an annual den/troop tradition; ask the leader to refer sibling troops.

Car Clubs, "First Friday Cruise Night"

Offer: Reserved parking lot for a monthly cruise night, featured-car spots, and group food specials for participants. A strong option if parking and permits allow.

Recurring mechanism: Standing monthly community night, co-promoted with the club. Confirm city permits/parking with the venue owner.

Local Businesses, "Team Lunch Perk"

Offer: Weekday group lunch rate + reserved tables with fast turnaround, or catering drop-off for the office.

Recurring mechanism: Standing monthly team lunch or recurring catering order; the office manager is the repeat contact.

Nonprofits, "Dine-to-Donate + Appreciation Meals"

Offer: % -of-sales fundraiser night (mirrors the school model) plus discounted board/volunteer appreciation meals.

Recurring mechanism: Quarterly fundraiser partnership with a named coordinator.

Contact Scripts

Personalize before sending, replace every bracketed placeholder. Keep cold emails to ~6–8 sentences / 50–125 words. Lead with their benefit, not the venue's features. Use the copy button on each template to grab the text.

Master Templates

1. Cold Outreach Email

Subject: A fun, easy [day-part] tradition for [Organization]

Hi [First Name],

I'm [Your Name] with [Venue Name], a fast food location here in Whittier. I work with local [segment, e.g., schools/teams/groups] to set up easy, affordable group visits, and I had [Organization] in mind.

For groups like yours we offer [one-line hook, e.g., "a reserved section and a flat per-person price during our quieter weekday mornings"]. It's turnkey on our end, you just show up and enjoy.

Would a quick 10-minute call this week work to see if it's a fit? Happy to hold a date that suits your schedule.

Warmly,
[Your Name] · [Venue Name] · [phone] · [email]

2. Follow-Up Email (send Day 4)

Subject: Re: A fun, easy [day-part] tradition for [Organization]

Hi [First Name],

Just floating this back to the top of your inbox. A lot of [segment] groups like the [hook] because it's genuinely zero hassle for the organizer, we handle everything.

Here's the short version: [1-sentence offer + benefit/number]. If it's easier, reply with a day that works and I'll send two time options.

Thanks!
[Your Name] · [Venue Name] · [phone]

3. Phone / Voicemail Script

Live call: "Hi, is this [First Name]? This is [Your Name] over at [Venue Name], our fast food location in Whittier. I'll be quick, we set up easy [day-part] group visits for [segment], and I'd love to set one up for [Organization]. Do you have two minutes, or is there a better time to reach you?"

(If yes → ask 1–2 questions: typical group size? best day-part? → propose a specific date.)

Voicemail (≤20 sec): "Hi [First Name], this is [Your Name] at [Venue Name] in Whittier. We do easy, affordable [day-part] group visits for [segment] and I'd love to set one up for [Organization]. Give me a call back at [phone], or I'll follow up by email. Thanks!"

4. In-Person / DM Pitch (2 sentences)

"We're [Venue Name] in Whittier, a fast food location, and we set up easy [day-part] group visits for [segment] with [one-line hook]. Could I grab your email to send a couple of dates?"

Outreach Cadence

A 5-touch sequence over ~16 days is the sweet spot for local group outreach, persistent without being pushy. Most positive replies arrive at Touch 3–5, so don't quit early. Space touches 3–4 days apart, vary the channel, and send emails Tuesday–Thursday, early-to-mid day.

  1. Touch 1 · Day 1 · Email (cold)

    Introduce the offer, ask for a 10-minute call. Personalize the subject + first line; send Tue–Thu AM.

  2. Touch 2 · Day 4 · Email (follow-up)

    Re-surface the offer; restate the one-line benefit. Reply on the same thread ("Re:").

  3. Touch 3 · Day 8 · Phone / voicemail

    Human touch; book a date live. If voicemail, leave a ≤20-second message and note you'll email.

  4. Touch 4 · Day 11 · Email or DM

    New angle, peer proof or a seasonal tie-in ("Other [segment] groups nearby are booking [month] dates").

  5. Touch 5 · Day 16 · "Breakup" email / call

    Polite close; leave the door open ("I'll stop here for now, reply anytime and I'll hold a date").

After Touch 5 with no reply

Move the prospect to Nurture in the tracker. Re-engage only on a trigger, new school year, sports season start, summer camp planning, club officer change, or a holiday/community-event theme. The nurture pool becomes the richest source of bookings over time.

Converting a First Booking → Recurring

The first visit is the audition. Lock the standing arrangement before they leave the building:

  1. Ask on-site, at peak happiness. While the group is smiling over milkshakes: "Should we make this your standing [weekly/monthly] spot?"
  2. Offer a "Host of the Month" perk. The organizer who books a recurring date gets a free meal, a reserved booth, or a small comp each visit.
  3. Default to a calendar series, not a one-off. Put the next date on the books immediately ("Same time next month?").
  4. Loyalty for organizers. A simple punch/stamp: every Nth group visit earns a free round of shakes or a comped table.
  5. Capture a testimonial + referral. Ask the happy organizer for a one-line quote and "who else runs a [segment] group like yours?"
  6. Send a 24-hour thank-you with the next confirmed date in writing.

Tracking & Workflow

Keep it simple. A shared Google Sheet works on day one; graduate to a free/low-cost CRM (HubSpot Free, Airtable) only if volume demands it. The rule that matters most: every prospect has a next action with a date.

KPIs & Success Metrics

Track these monthly, and review the leading indicators weekly.

Activity (Leading)

  • New prospects added
  • Outreach touches sent (by channel)
  • Calls connected / meetings booked

Conversion (The Funnel)

  • Contact → Engaged rate (~10–20% healthy benchmark)
  • Engaged → First Booking rate
  • First Booking → Recurring rate — the single most important metric

Outcome (Lagging)

  • # of active recurring group arrangements
  • Off-peak covers generated by groups
  • Group revenue / month & avg revenue per recurring group
  • Fundraiser nights hosted + estimated incremental traffic
  • Referrals generated per happy organizer

Suggested first-90-day targets (adjust with the venue owner)

  • 40+ prospects contacted/month
  • 6–10 first bookings/month
  • ≥40% of first bookings converted to a recurring arrangement
  • 8–12 active recurring groups by end of Q1
